About

My career started with a lie.

Chris Issa · Producer, engineer, manager

I didn't have a studio, gear, or clients. None of the things you're supposed to have. Just a laptop, some ideas, and a stranger who asked me if I knew a place to produce an album.

I said, yes, me.

I had nothing. But I took the money, called my father, and told him we were building a studio, because that's the lie I'd just sold. 72 hours later, I didn't have to lie anymore. The studio existed.

That's who I am.

Producer, sound engineer, artist manager. Everything I've built has been on the back of saying yes before I was ready, then figuring it out fast enough that nobody noticed.

Nobody taught me this industry. No mentor pulled me aside. No one handed me a roadmap or told me what to do when a client shows up with a dream and a deadline. I read the books. I watched the interviews. I listened to every podcast I could find. I learned the legal side, the business side, and the creative side, not because I wanted to, but because nobody else was going to do it for me.

Somewhere in the middle of all that, I realised something. The artist shouldn't have to carry any of this. They shouldn't be their own manager and their own lawyer and their own booking agent and their own marketing department. They shouldn't be drowning in the parts of the industry that have nothing to do with the music. That weight kills careers before they start, and I've watched it happen.

Every artist I work with is a chance to be the person I needed when I started.

When I was starting out, I needed someone in my corner. Someone who knew the path, or was at least honest enough to say, I don't know this part yet, but we'll figure it out together. I didn't have that. I think about that version of me a lot. Ideas and no direction, potential and no structure, hunger and nobody to channel it.

I'm not here to take a percentage and disappear. I'm not here to tell you what you want to hear. I'm here to walk with you from wherever you are now to wherever the work deserves to go. Production, engineering, release, strategy, all of it. If I hit something I don't know yet, I'll tell you, and then I'll go learn it.

That's the only promise I can make, and it's the only one that matters.
